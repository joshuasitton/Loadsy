**Monday 14 September to Friday 25 September 2026.** Ten working days.

**Goal:** Loadsy is ready to submit to the App Store. The last remaining action is
pressing *Submit for Review*.

Nothing in this file is a commitment until the Chairman makes it one. Decisions

## Definition of done

"Ready to submit" is easy to claim and hard to mean, so it is pinned here. All of
these, or the sprint has not met its goal:

1. **A production build is in App Store Connect**, uploaded through EAS, processed
   with no errors, and installed from TestFlight.
2. **It has run end to end on a physical iPhone with real detection** – photograph a
   room, get an inventory, a truck size and prices – not on mocks and not in a
   simulator.
3. **Every App Store Connect field is complete:** name, subtitle, description,
   keywords, category, age rating, support URL, privacy policy URL, the privacy
   nutrition label, export compliance, screenshots, and notes for the reviewer.
4. **No known rejection risk is outstanding.** Each one found below is either fixed
   or deliberately accepted in writing.

### Week one – a correct, safe release

**Mon 14 – Tue 15**

- ~~**E1 · Land the branches.**~~ **Done 14 September.** #2 (error handling), #3 (the new
  mark), #4 (real photo-quality checks, landed per decision 4, plus these docs) and #5 (the
  eval measuring the shipped app), all as merge commits. `main` builds with `jpeg-js`
  installed, and lint, typecheck and 307 tests pass on it.
  One slip, recovered: deleting #2's branch closed #3, which was stacked on it, rather than
  retargeting it. #3 was reopened by restoring its base at the already-merged commit and
  pointing it at `main`. Lesson: retarget a stacked PR *before* deleting its base.

**Tue 15 – Wed 16**

- **E2 · Make detection reliable.** Set `thinking` and `effort` explicitly in
  `/v1/detect`, give `max_tokens` headroom, and handle `stop_reason` – including a
  refusal – instead of reporting every failure as unparseable JSON.
  *Done when* a four-photo request returns valid JSON well inside the 11-second timeout,
  measured with a handful of real calls (a few cents).
  *Waits on* the key (decision 2).
  **In progress, 15 September.** The first live run on real photos (four of a family room)
  proved the failure: thinking left at Opus 5's default spent the whole 4,000-token budget
  and 60 seconds and returned nothing. Thinking is now off by default with an 8,000-token
  budget, and the prompt now splits sectionals into their sections, ignores furniture
  seen through into other rooms, counts small belongings as boxes, and asks only for the
  fields the app reads – each answering an error measured on that room. Still open: a
  complete answer took **64 seconds against the 11-second timeout**. The shorter format
  should cut that substantially but is unlikely to reach 11 seconds for a full room, so
  the timeout itself needs a decision – a longer wait with progress, streaming, or a
  faster model – and `stop_reason` handling in the route is not done.

- ~~**E2b · Ask for ceiling height.**~~ **Done 14 September**, ahead of E2. Once per move,
  before the first photo: "Are your ceilings the standard 8 ft high?" If no, quick choices –
  7, 9, 10, 12 ft – or "Other" in feet, which reads `9'6"` and refuses what it can't read
  rather than guessing. The camera stays off until it is answered, as it does for an
  unnamed room; once answered it folds to one line with a way to change it.
  A non-standard height is told to the model in the user message; the system prompt is
  untouched, so its cacheable prefix is too. All three done-when conditions are pinned by
  tests: 9 ft sends "108 in"; "yes" or no answer sends the request byte for byte as before,
  checked against `main` for 1–4 photos; and the eval passes `ceilingFt` from ground truth.
  Checked in the browser at phone size: the flow, a reload keeping the answer, 44pt targets,
  no overflow, and screen-reader state – which needed `aria-checked` set directly, because
  react-native-web drops `accessibilityState.checked` and `.selected`.

- **E3 · Make "discarded" true.** Delete the cache files that `prepareUpload` and
  `measureFrame` write, once they have been read.
  *Done when* the cache holds no photo files after a capture. The capture screen's
  sentence, and `APP_STORE.md`, both depend on it.

- **E4 · Protect the endpoint.** Add per-client rate limiting to `/v1/detect`. Give
  production its own deployment, so a demo deploy can no longer replace what installed
  apps call.
  *Done when* repeated calls are refused and a demo deploy leaves production untouched.
  Note: in-memory counters are weak on serverless hosting, which is why the spend limit in
  decision 2 is the real backstop. App Attest is the proper fix, and belongs in v1.1.

## Risks

In order of how badly each would miss the goal.

1. **The current app on a phone.** Installing is proven; everything added in the 16
   commits since 27 August, and real detection, is not. Camera, permissions, safe areas
   and the solver's speed on a phone are all untested. E7 is placed early for this reason.
2. **Open endpoint cost.** Mitigated by the spend limit (hard cap) and E4 (rate limiting).
   Not solved until App Attest.
3. **Review rejection** for unavailable features (guideline 2.1). Mitigated by E5, if
   decision 5 goes as recommended.
4. **Unmeasured accuracy.** Not a submission blocker, but the product's central promise.
5. **The "Data Not Collected" answer** depends on Anthropic's retention terms. Decision 6.
6. **The app's name.** Not reserved until the App Store Connect record exists. Decision 1.
7. **Dependency drift** – Expo reports 12 packages behind. Aligned early in E5 so a late
   upgrade can't break the final build.

Retired on 14 September: **Apple enrolment**, previously the top risk – the Chairman is
enrolled.

## Out of scope for this sprint

- **The 360° sweep** – v1.1, decided 14 September.
- **Premium payments.** No StoreKit this sprint.
- **Affiliate agreements** – but start the applications. They take time, and the
  Sweep Capture Decision memo shows they fund the free tier either way.
- **Android, iPad, and component tests.**
- **App Attest** – v1.1, as the proper fix for the open endpoint.
- **A decision step for loose small items.** Decided 15 September: small belongings are
  counted as boxes, and anything too big for a large box (18 × 18 × 24 in) – a tall lamp,
  a tower fan, a dog bed, a doormat – stays on the list as its own item, to be put to the
  user later for a decision on how to handle it. The rule is in the prompt now; the
  decision step is a new screen, and it waits until after submission. On day one those
  items appear in the inventory review like everything else, where they can already be
  edited or removed.
